# Positioning research (R-004)


**Plan references:** §17.5 R-004, §10 (differentiation).
**Review status:** approved — positioning is strategic copy; sign-off is tracked in `Q-0008-strategic-copy`.

The purpose is to sharpen differentiation and avoid category clichés, not to
copy competitors. The clichés below are enforced at build time by
`forbiddenCopy.ts` where a keyword can catch them; the rest are Gate A
human-review calls. Each defensible difference is anchored to a
`whyHelix` manifesto pillar, and each response ships clean of forbidden copy.

## Subjects researched (§17.5)

- **Enterprise-value creation** — How credible firms describe the value they create, and how they attribute it, so the $500m+ headline reads as evidence rather than a boast.
- **Operating leverage** — How firms describe turning effort into disproportionate, durable results, without collapsing into abstraction.
- **Performance-linked fees** — How firms describe aligned economics and gain-share without promising outcomes they cannot control.
- **Venture building** — How studios describe co-building companies, and the generic venture-studio language Helix must not fall into.
- **Deep product and technology partnership** — How firms describe embedded, hands-on partnership, and how to keep it distinct from staffing.

## Reference firms and their language patterns

### Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)

- **Archetype:** platform venture capital
- **Language pattern:** Positions the firm as an operating platform with in-house functions that “help founders” across a large portfolio.
- **What Helix should avoid:** Implying a deep services bench and portfolio scale; Helix is a small, selective firm that takes on a handful of situations at a time.

### Sequoia Capital

- **Archetype:** elite venture capital
- **Language pattern:** Mission and legacy framing — helping the daring build enduring, legendary companies.
- **What Helix should avoid:** Grand legacy language unbacked by named, verifiable outcomes; the tone system demands proof before theatre.

### McKinsey (incl. Leap and RTS)

- **Archetype:** strategy consultancy
- **Language pattern:** Category-defining consultancy vocabulary: digital transformation, end-to-end solutions, capability building.
- **What Helix should avoid:** The exact consultancy clichés the tone system bans; these read as generic and undifferentiated.

### Bain Capital portfolio operations

- **Archetype:** private-equity operating partners
- **Language pattern:** Value-creation teams framed around “results, not reports” and hands-on operating support.
- **What Helix should avoid:** Control-ownership and balance-sheet framing; Helix aligns economics without buying control of the business.

### Alvarez & Marsal

- **Archetype:** operating and performance advisers
- **Language pattern:** Action-and-results framing built on operational turnaround and performance improvement.
- **What Helix should avoid:** Distress and turnaround connotations; Helix partners on upside, not rescue.

### eFounders / Hexa

- **Archetype:** startup studio
- **Language pattern:** Co-founding language — the studio builds startups alongside founders and takes founding equity.
- **What Helix should avoid:** Generic venture-studio self-description; §17.2 and §15.3 warn against sounding like every other studio.

### Blackstone portfolio operations

- **Archetype:** private-equity value-creation team
- **Language pattern:** Dedicated operating professionals framed as driving measurable portfolio value creation.
- **What Helix should avoid:** Implying capital deployment and portfolio scale Helix does not have; the difference is a shared value thesis, not a balance sheet.

  - The plan bans this only “when unsupported by detail”; a scan cannot judge whether the surrounding copy earns it, so it stays a Gate A human-review call.

## Where Helix has a defensible difference

- **We take a position, not a brief.** — Helix forms its own view of the opportunity and what must become true for value to be created, rather than executing a client's brief the way an adviser or agency does.
- **We share the risk and the upside.** — Helix is paid as it delivers, with upside tied to the value thesis playing out — aligned economics without taking control of the business the way a private-equity owner does.
- **We operate from inside the team.** — Helix works from inside the team against shared objectives, not as an adviser who hands over a deck and disappears or a staffing arrangement billed by the seat.

## Objections and copy responses

### Every firm claims to “create enterprise value”. How is $500m+ actually attributable to you rather than the market or the founders?

Fair challenge — that is why every figure on this site resolves to a documented claim with an attribution standard and evidence behind it. We publish how the result was achieved, not just the number, and we are deliberately conservative about what we claim.

### “Operating leverage” is a buzzword. What actually changes operationally when you engage?

We build the independent case, align the incentives, join the team to deliver, and then make the result sustainable and hand it back. Each stage is concrete work, not a slide.

### Performance-linked fees just mean you cherry-pick the easy wins and load the fixed fee.

We take on a small number of situations where there is a credible path to materially higher value, and we are paid as we deliver, with upside tied to that thesis. The incentives are built to point in the same direction as yours.

### Venture studios spread themselves thin across a portfolio. How do I know I get real attention?

We are selective by design and operate from inside one team at a time, not across a portfolio. That is the point of taking a position rather than filling a roster.

### “Embedded partnership” is just staffing with a nicer name.

We take a position and own a shared value thesis with you — not a scope of seats billed by the hour. We work alongside your team against shared objectives, and we win when the business wins.
